# FDE Track: The FDE Résumé and Its Six Signals

Source: `roles-this-book-trains`, section `appendix-a-the-fde-r-sum-six-signals`. Everything below comes from that page. The page notes that interview and screening practices shift quickly, and that this appendix was verified against sources from mid-2026. It cites its screening analysis to its own footnote (20), which I have not checked independently.

## 1. What it is

Recruiters do not read an FDE profile the way they read a software engineer's. The page says analysis of real screening practice converges on **six signals**, and a profile that buries them "gets rejected before the technical bar is ever tested."

The signals split into two groups of three.

**Have you actually delivered?**
1. **Shipped production systems.** Real deployments, not features on a team's backlog.
2. **Quantifiable impact.** Not "built feature X" but what the customer gained, in numbers.
3. **Direct customer exposure.** You sat with the stakeholder, not behind a product manager.

**How do you deliver?**
4. **Messy-data work.** Real client environments are never clean.
5. **Ownership under ambiguity.** You ran the project when no one defined it.
6. **AI/LLM depth.** RAG, agents and evals, "the reason the role exists."

Three rewrites follow from the signals:
- Reframe every bullet from **activity to outcome**.
- Write **"I," not "we."**
- **Cut competitive-programming awards** and replace them with a **portfolio**.

## 2. Why it works that way

- **The screen tests the role, not generic engineering.** An FDE is accountable for making customer-specific work function in production (see the earlier lessons from this page). Each signal is evidence of that accountability: deployed, measured, in front of the customer, in dirty data, without a defined brief, with AI skills.
- **"We" reads as "was carried."** The page says FDE screeners read "we" as the candidate being carried by a team, and they probe for it in the hiring-manager round. First person claims ownership that can be tested.
- **Puzzle prizes signal the wrong preparation.** A prize for solving data-structure puzzles "signals preparation for the wrong interview." This matches the interview loop, where no LeetCode appeared in the documented live-build loop.
- **The portfolio is the credential.** The page cites a rule from its freelance section: examples are a deployed Worker, a shipped plugin, or a live connector app, each with a one-line outcome attached. The page says every capstone in the book is designed to be exactly that line.
- **A governed slice of a profession is the standout item.** The page says a vendor-neutral candidate should lead with "a governed slice of a profession, published for both readers." A deployed Worker proves you can build. A governed slice proves you own something no employer handed you, and the page says a screener has almost certainly never seen one.

## 3. What breaks without it

- A profile that buries the six signals is rejected before anyone tests your technical skill.
- Activity-style bullets ("built an ETL pipeline") give the screener no outcome to credit, so the delivery signals are missing.
- "We" language triggers probing in the hiring-manager round and can make you look carried.
- Competitive-programming awards signal preparation for the wrong interview.
- Without a portfolio of shipped artifacts, there is no proof of the first three signals.

## 4. Worked example

The page's own rewrite, annotated:

- **Before (activity):** "Built an ETL pipeline."
- **After (outcome, first person):** "I shipped a pipeline that cut the client's month-end close from five days to two."

What changed:
- "Shipped" covers signal 1 (production system).
- "Five days to two" covers signal 2 (quantifiable impact, in the customer's terms).
- "The client's" implies a real customer, which supports signal 3.
- "I" removes the "was carried" reading.

The portfolio line works the same way: "a deployed Worker, with a one-line outcome attached."

## 5. Quiz

**Q1. Which of these is one of the three signals that ask whether you have actually delivered?**
A. Competitive-programming awards
B. Direct customer exposure
C. Familiarity with many programming languages
D. A long list of team projects

**Q2. According to the page, how do FDE screeners read "we" on a résumé?**
A. As a sign of strong teamwork
B. As a neutral style choice
C. As "was carried," something they probe for in the hiring-manager round
D. As proof of customer exposure

**Q3. Which bullet best follows the page's rewrite rules?**
A. "We built an ETL pipeline for a client."
B. "Responsible for data engineering tasks."
C. "I shipped a pipeline that cut the client's month-end close from five days to two."
D. "Won a regional algorithms contest."

**Q4. Why does the page say competitive-programming awards should be cut?**
A. They are too hard to verify
B. They signal preparation for the wrong interview
C. Recruiters are not allowed to read them
D. They show too much customer exposure

### Answers

1. **B.** Shipped production systems, quantifiable impact and direct customer exposure are the "delivered" group.
2. **C.** The page says screeners read "we" as "was carried."
3. **C.** It moves from activity to outcome, uses "I," and gives numbers.
4. **B.** The page says a prize for solving data-structure puzzles "signals preparation for the wrong interview."
